CHATHURIKA JAYANI

Chathurika Jayani, a graduate of the University of Visual and Performing Arts in Colombo, has gained recognition both locally and internationally for her innovative mixed-media art. Her works have been exhibited at prestigious venues such as the Saskia Fernando Gallery, Paradise Road Galleries, and the Asian Art Biennale, earning her numerous awards at the State Art and Sculpture Festival, including first place in 2011–2012 and 2024.

Jayani's practice is defined by her use of unconventional materials—corrugated board, handmade paper, and threads—crafted with tools like needles and gas torches. Through bold colors and layered textures, she explores themes of urbanization, industrialization, and human resilience, often through a distinctly female perspective. Her early series such as City and New Works reflected on urban transformation, while The Secret Vows delved into the inner conflicts between identity, emotion, and societal expectations. Later, in Dreamscape (2022), she merged reality with imagination, portraying chaotic yet dreamlike cityscapes that question progress, identity, and environmental sustainability.

Her large-scale installations at Cinnamon Life, including Dream Paradise and Dream Paradise Odyssey, further expand on these themes by juxtaposing Sri Lanka's natural and cultural heritage with symbols of industrial growth. These works critique rapid development while celebrating the nation's evolving identity. Deeply personal yet universally resonant, Jayani's art chronicles her journey as a woman balancing multiple roles—artist, mother, teacher—and serves as a poetic reflection on hope, devotion, and the enduring optimism of women worldwide.

DREAM PARADISE - ODYSSEY

'Dream Paradise' is my visionary collection that encapsulates the essence of Sri Lanka. Each work is a tribute to a distinct district, where I intricately weave together the human behaviors, aspirations, and dreams that shape our nation's diverse cultural and social identity.

Through my evocative interpretations of landscapes, traditions, and narratives, I distill the soul of each region. From the luminous coastal edges to the ethereal highland vistas, I capture the island's rhythms, offering a profound reflection on its multifaceted beauty and complexity.

This body of work extends beyond visual storytelling—I invite audiences on an introspective journey through Sri Lanka's vibrant and interconnected spirit. For me, Dream Paradise transcends mere depiction, emerging as an eloquent testament to the unity within diversity and the indomitable.

DREAM PARADISE

In Dream Paradise, Chathurika Jayani reimagines Sri Lanka as a living mosaic — a surreal landscape where memory, aspiration, and urban flux converge. Her canvases, crafted from acrylic, auto paint, recycled plastic, and woolen thread, pulse with intricate textures and vivid energy. They blur the boundaries between the handmade and the industrial, the real and the imagined. Each panel becomes a world in motion — cities dissolve into clouds, mountains hum with color, and homes drift like fragments of dreams suspended in time.

Jayani's compositions echo both the chaos and tenderness of Sri Lankan life: the hum of its streets, the resilience of its people, and the fragile beauty of coexistence amidst constant change. Thread and texture act as emotional seams, binding together places and people — coastlines shimmer beside misty hills, and everyday dwellings rise into fantastical skies. The result is an allegorical paradise — one born not of perfection, but of persistence, complexity, and collective imagination. Dream Paradise is less a map of Sri Lanka than a state of mind — an introspective terrain where belonging, nostalgia, and transformation intertwine in luminous, restless harmony.

DREAMSCAPES

Chathurika Jayani's Dreamscapes consist of heavy set colours and textures depicting a fantastical urban situation. While the artist's work explores themes of urbanisation and city building, her painted structures, which could be described as ahistorical, often refer to the omnipresent and corrugated tiling sheets found on buildings across the country. Her approach is a conceptual one, where the architectural subject is observed and painted from several perspectives, including the literal and the abstract. Through these conceptual structures, Chathurika participates in a global conversation about the current state of architecture and urban planning.

The architectural facades in her paintings include playful geometries that reflect the often vivid nature of proposed redevelopment projects. Her conceptual buildings invite the viewer to engage in multiple readings as these colourful conglomerates tend to hover above and mask the vibrant but darkly lit cityscapes and their densely packed communities on the ground below. Underlying the majority of these works is a foreboding reflection on the relationship between urbanism and the environment. Whereas the idea of floating cities in the sky, spurred on by the speed of engineering and technology, may be awe-inspiring, Chathurika's paintings question the moral conduct of these extensive construction projects. What are the terms of their legacy?

THE SECRET VOWS

What influenced my series of paintings,'The Secret Vows' was the beauty of the prayer flags of various shapes and colours used in religious culture in the Asian region. These flags were used as a symbol of either a vow or a hope and these vows are made for a better future than the present that is being experienced. But only person who really knows what the vow or the hope is, is the person who makes it.

The prayer flag tradition has a long continuous history dating back to ancient Tibet, China, Persia, India and Sri Lanka. The meanings behind prayer flag texts and symbols, indeed behind the whole idea of prayer flags, are based on the most profound concepts of religious belief and philosophy.

ESCAPES

The word Escapes is argumentative and has different perceptions, which are based on personal aspects. In this series of paintings, my attempt was to capture bolts of freedom in the lives of people who live very simple lives, in my own way. Though they lack facilities, luxuries or wealth, their atmosphere is full of colors. Their shelters, clothes and festive decorations are, despite this, bright and colorful. These colors are placed over them, wrapping a part of their life that is otherwise left in darkness.

CITY
